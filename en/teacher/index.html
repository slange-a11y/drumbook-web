For drum teachers

They practise at home. They just don't know what.

The most honest answer to "so, did you practise?" is usually a shrug. Not because anyone is lying — but because after a week nobody remembers how long, on what, and at which tempo.

Drumbook is a practice diary the student keeps. You set nothing up and administer nobody. You get something in your hand.

What you hold in your hand at the next lesson

A PDF report the student produces with two taps — covering exactly the period since your last lesson, if they want.

  • Every practice session with date, length and exercise — and for each exercise the tempo reached, along with their own verdict.
  • Actual and target tempo side by side: "96 of 130" tells you in one line what ten minutes of asking does not.
  • The student's notes — "above 100 it turns heavy". That is exactly where you start the lesson.
  • The questions that came up while practising. They arrive with three concrete questions instead of "erm".
  • Songs stay out of it. The report shows what is practised — not what was played on the side.

Your instruction survives the week

What you set is entered into the lesson by the student, along with your assessment. Those exercises go into their library and get priority when the practice list is put together. The sentence that mattered is still there on Wednesday.

You don't need an iPhone

No device, no installation, no account, no teacher edition, no training. Drumbook runs on the student's device; you get the report however suits you — printed on paper or by email. Whether you own an iPhone yourself makes no difference.

No student data on your side

Anyone teaching minors thinks twice about which platform to recommend. Here there is no sign-in, no server and no student data with you — everything stays with the student, on their device and in their own iCloud. You get a PDF, nothing else.

What Drumbook is not: not a teaching platform, not a class register, not a portal with student administration. There is no shared access and no overview across several students — that would be a completely different app. Drumbook turns one student into someone who knows what they have practised. And it is still being tested: built by one person who is learning the drums, in daily use since 2 September. What is finished — and what is not →
